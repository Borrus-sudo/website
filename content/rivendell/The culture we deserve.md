If I ever end up managing a team, these are the principles that I think I should follow:
- Small, effective team granting lots of agency to its members. A sweet number of 12? https://jmulholland.com/small-group/. Also [[2026-10-07#^ba6449]]
- Having a common high level goal without getting fixated on the "how". As long as there is a shared goal, the way we get there does not matter.
- The corollary of the above point is to explore multiple paths of achieving the same thing to avoid going all-in on one approach.
- Focus on interdisciplinary people over interdisciplinary teams. Esp useful in the AI age.
- Problem rich environment is a must. If the work done is revolutionary, you can make a new field out of it! It is tough to formulate problems, questions. https://stephango.com/solving-problem-finding is the toughest. 
- alice maz's [article](https://alicemaz.substack.com/p/you-can-just-do-stuff): The salient properties of the research labs like ARPA and PARC:
	- vision: grand unifying vision that everyone is aligned with
	- people: smart, motivated people deeply aligned with the vision
	- intensity: gotta work hard
	- lawlessness: a very flat structure. people can just collaborate with each others. ideas are shared freely. good ideas get developed and bad ideas wither. folks aren't micromanaged on what they want to work on. They are free to pursue questions of their choice.  
	- speed: be lightning fast
	- failure: big shots can lead to failure. give researcher the space to fail. This really matters when money is involved in the equation.
	- purity: people are given the agency to work on anything they want. no micromanagement. there are no business objectives to pursue.
	
  Above are the ways to build a good culture. Such a culture is also a form of environment based learning. The environment such represents a space with the necessary ethos require to do great work encoded in its fabric. 
  The way to commercialize something is to sift through the research generated and then look at something with the eyes of product development. anything else can lead to disaster. invest money in talent.
  Letting funders pick winners on the basis of merit will choke speculative work and only encourage incrementalism. Purely invest in talent. 
 
  > articulate a vision, or find someone else who has laid one out that you can believe in. find the best people and become close to them. challenge each other to keep up, be the best possible versions of yourselves. work hard, work fast, pour as much of yourself into the work as you are able to. disregard rules as much as you can without seriously jeopardizing your life or liberty. and don't worry about a practical outcome. just aim to do the biggest, most important thing you can
