	- Essay:
		- Ideas are like clouds of enlightenment that swirl around your head which threaten to disappear themselves in the light of _stultitia_. Hence we are driven by the constant need re-visit them in our heads so that we don't lose them. Having a low friction log to quickly store them is a huge cognitive easer. Additionally we should regularly spend time to re-visit them, think and write about them in a detailed fashion. This helps us to crystallize those clouds of thoughts into a well structured notion, which upon re-calling allows us to re-live that moment of enlightenment when we first discovered them. 
		- The process of writing enables us to grow our original fragile idea into a concrete powerful concept whilst also serving us as a valuable piece of reference for our future self. However writing about that idea simply does not allow us to use it effectively. To truly internalize the idea into our minds, we need to form a habit that will reflexively pop that idea into our head when the need arises. To achieve this we need some sort of system in place to re-visit upon our old ideas and stay in touch with them. Failing to do so makes us a victim of the [[The Collector's Fallacy]]. Having a textual artifact about the idea makes it easy to re-visit and recall our moment of enlightenment. These textual artifacts serve as [[evergreen notes]]. The [[The What, The How, The Why and its TL;DR]] is a model in which I like to structure essays about my ideas
		- Henrik Carlson writes in his blog, _Escaping Flatland_ :
			> During the first two centuries of the Roman Empire, there spread a practice known as hypomnēmata, a type of notetaking system, used as a tool for meditation,1 in which the writer would store quotes from books they had read. Each day, often in the morning, the notetaker would open their notebook and look for a passage relevant to something they were struggling with, and then they would meditate on that—unpacking it, making the idea top of mind, ensuring it was alive in them. If they needed courage, for instance, they could meditate on an anecdote that made it real for them what it meant to act bravely. The idea was that over time, the insights they gathered by reading would be transformed into character, something deeply ingrained in their way of thinking and seeing and acting.
		- This idea is similar to the [[Spaced repetition system]] developed by [[Andy Matuschak]] which uses a scheduling algorithm allowing us to re-visit our old ideas and decides the interval of schedule based upon how well we are able to recollect it. The judgement is done on a basis of questions pre-defined by the user or generated by a LLM. Similar to [Ankify](https://www.ankify.app/) cards. 
		- Spending sometime in the morning to refer old ideas back as popped by the space repetition system or you choosing them is very powerful to deeply internalize them by forming [[Habit]]. To make this more efficient, having a **TL;DR** of every idea which instructs you the actions you need to take or the key principle you need to remember goes a far way in revising them. These TL;DRs can serve as [[Generative Mantra]]. The spec for writing a good TL;DR has been more elaborated in the essay [[The What, The How, The Why and its TL;DR]]
		- Like [[Zettelkasten Notes]] these ideas need to be densely connected with each other. The sources from which these ideas are inspired also have to be represented into the system so they can be referenced upon. I use [[Obsidian]] and [[mycelium]] for growing and tending my digital garden of well developed thoughts, observations, facts, inspiring literature from awesome people and a daily working log for storing all my half baked nascent ideas. 
		- **Evolution** is a really important style in my note-taking method. It is not possible to craft a polished mature essay from a nascent thought in the first pass. Hell most of my ideas are not "worthy" enough for further exploration. The goal is to collect every idea effortlessly, tend upon the useful ones, develop them in multiple passes and link them with other ideas, referenced work to create a dense rainforest of our work. Not every idea or observation deserves a well structured essay. Some are meaningfully small. However these might serve as a base for our future ambitious ideas. Having them in your system makes it easy for you to link them up later. 
		  Inspired from [[jzhao]] I label my notes as #seed, #fruit, #evergreen. #seed for everything that is its nascent stages. #fruit are projects which are a by-product of my #seed and #evergreen  essays. [[mycelium]] feature of time evolution allows me to quickly glance at the evolution of my thought process!
		- Again like [[Zettelkasten notes]] try to keep your notes as **atomic** as possible. Every notes should revolve around a central theme which might link up with other notes wherever necessary!
		- For storing and hoarding foreign pieces of literature scraped from the web, using Obsidian clipper is greatly helpful to store the useful parts into your _notes_ easily. It is necessary to write about the learning in your own words to properly internalize and understand them. This can again be done over several passes. 
		- To summarize my note taking process:
			- Everything in my digital garden is a note. 
			- Notes represent ideas, observations, awesome pieces of media you come across on the internet including but not limited to books, blog posts, audio, video etc!
			- Ideas are given a special importance. You need to store your ideas into a daily working log as you think of them. 
			- Make sure your notes are **atomic** and densely linked.
			- Use evolutionary strategy to take notes. 
			- Your digital garden should be accessible to other to get valuable feedback!
			- Spaced repetition helps me remember the important themes from my notes to help them in-grain in my mind. Structuring your takeaway as a [[Generative Mantra]] makes it amenable to form a habit out of and for quick recall.
